# D — WRITING, EDITORIAL QUALITY, AND ARTICLE STRUCTURE

Load this category only when the article task, affected runtime boundary, or release risk selects it. The directives below are exact authorized requirements. Record applicability and live evidence in the adopting repository; policy presence is not runtime verification.

- **D01.** Define the reader, their starting knowledge, the question they need answered, and the concrete outcome the article should enable before choosing its outline.
- **D02.** Deliver the central answer early, followed by evidence, reasoning, examples, exceptions, and the action the reader can take.
- **D03.** Write a precise title that matches the article's actual promise and search intent; avoid exaggerated claims, unsupported superlatives, and manufactured urgency.
- **D04.** Use an informative deck and short takeaway section that help readers decide whether the page solves their problem without duplicating the whole introduction.
- **D05.** Require at least 2,000 substantive editorial words for a new generated article, normally 2,000–3,000; extend for useful depth and choose a deeper angle instead of padding a narrow topic.
- **D06.** Build each section around a distinct reader question. Give headings meaningful descriptions instead of repeating the target keyword with minor variations.
- **D07.** Use direct, concrete language and active verbs. Delete generic AI introductions, ornamental adjectives, repetitive summaries, and vague claims about transformation.
- **D08.** Synthesize sources into an argument or explanation; do not produce a sequence of loosely connected summaries of the first search results.
- **D09.** Include an original contribution appropriate to the topic, such as a reproducible example, decision rubric, dataset transformation, practical template, or carefully reasoned synthesis.
- **D10.** Show a worked example with realistic, clearly labeled assumptions; include intermediate reasoning needed to reproduce the outcome without inventing real-world results.
- **D11.** Explain tradeoffs and unsuitable use cases. A recommendation should state whom it serves, what it costs or requires, and when another choice would be better.
- **D12.** Keep comparisons fair by using consistent criteria, versions, dates, units, and test conditions; separate measured performance from advertised capability.
- **D13.** Explain specialist terminology on first meaningful use and provide a contextual glossary only when it materially improves comprehension.
- **D14.** Treat technical tutorials as executable guidance: test the central example in a suitable sandbox, record versions, redact secrets, and verify the resulting behavior.
- **D15.** Put a source beside each factual claim during composition; do not draft unsupported assertions and manufacture a bibliography after the fact.
- **D16.** Use brief paragraphs, purposeful lists, examples, and tables according to information structure; avoid turning every section into an identical card grid.
- **D17.** Maintain narrative continuity between components. Introduce a chart or tool, explain its conclusion, and connect it to the decision rather than dropping it into the page.
- **D18.** Prefer one clear explanation to repeated variants. Run a section-level duplication pass across the article and against the site's existing content.
- **D19.** Make numerical claims readable: identify timeframe, currency, population, units, baseline, and uncertainty at the point of use.
- **D20.** Include common mistakes and recovery steps where they prevent reader failure; derive them from evidence, actual experience, or clearly explained reasoning.
- **D21.** Add FAQs only for useful questions not already answered clearly. Avoid repeating keyword variants or treating an FAQ block as a guaranteed rich-result tactic.
- **D22.** Include a concise conclusion that follows from the evidence and a practical next step; avoid repeating every heading as a closing summary.
- **D23.** Use descriptive internal links to genuinely helpful related pages, with contextual anchor text and a logical next-reading sequence.
- **D24.** Fit the article's voice to the site and reader while maintaining the same standards of clarity, factual accuracy, and honest attribution across brands.
- **D25.** Attribute real authorship and review accurately. An organizational byline is preferable to an invented expert, fabricated credential, or fictional firsthand account.
- **D26.** Disclose relevant AI assistance, sponsorship, affiliate relationships, and supplied materials in an appropriate editorial context; do not make the article feel like an internal system log.
- **D27.** Create distinct drafting, evidence-review, and copy-editing passes with explicit goals; apply edits as reviewable changes and preserve valid citations.
- **D28.** Evaluate originality and usefulness directly. Do not use an AI-detector score, readability score, word count, or keyword count as the definition of quality.
- **D29.** Read the assembled page aloud or use an equivalent cadence pass to catch awkward transitions, repetition, excessive sentence length, and discontinuity between prose and widgets.
- **D30.** Finish only when the article answers its question completely, supports its facts, adds value beyond its sources, and passes the measurable publishing gates.
