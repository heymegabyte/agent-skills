# E — VISUAL APPLICATIONS: 30 PURPOSEFUL WAYS TO EXPLAIN AN ARTICLE

Load this category only when the article task, affected runtime boundary, or release risk selects it. The directives below are exact authorized requirements. Record applicability and live evidence in the adopting repository; policy presence is not runtime verification.

- **E01.** Thesis cover — GENERATED ART / HYBRID. Commission one memorable visual metaphor that expresses the article’s central argument in the configured brand style. Keep meaningful titles, labels, logos, and citations in accessible HTML or deterministic overlays.
- **E02.** Reader scenario vignette — GENERATED ART. Illustrate the concrete situation the intended reader faces, using the same visual language as the cover. Identify invented scenarios as illustrations; never imply the scene depicts an actual customer or documented event.
- **E03.** Conceptual cutaway — HYBRID. Use generated artwork to make an abstract mechanism approachable, then place accurate labels and explanatory callouts with SVG or HTML. Label conceptual illustrations clearly and validate every technical relationship against cited sources.
- **E04.** Annotated product capture — AUTHENTIC CAPTURE / HYBRID. Capture the real product state being discussed, then add numbered callouts tied to the explanation. Record product version and capture date, redact sensitive details, and never fabricate interface evidence.
- **E05.** Comparable before and after — AUTHENTIC CAPTURE / DETERMINISTIC DATA. Show a real transformation using matched framing, scale, and conditions. Explain what changed and cite the measurements; label illustrative mockups explicitly when authentic comparison material is unavailable.
- **E06.** Process walkthrough — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Convert a difficult procedure into a numbered flow with clear inputs, decisions, and outputs. Preserve an equivalent ordered text explanation, source material steps, and avoid visual arrows that imply unsupported dependencies.
- **E07.** Decision tree — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Help readers choose an option through explicit, evidence-based questions and outcomes. Include the underlying decision rules as text, identify judgment calls, and provide a usable mobile layout without horizontal panning.
- **E08.** Architecture or dependency map — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Explain how systems, concepts, or services connect through a compact diagram. Distinguish data flow, ownership, and dependencies visually; derive labels from verified documentation and expose a text equivalent.
- **E09.** Causal mechanism model — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Draw the proposed mechanism connecting an intervention and outcome. Distinguish demonstrated effects, associations, and hypotheses using labels and line styles; cite each relationship and never present correlation as causation.
- **E10.** Historical or implementation timeline — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Place verified milestones on a dated timeline, or show a proposed schedule with its assumptions. Keep historical facts distinct from forecasts and include readable dates and sources outside the artwork.
- **E11.** Geographic context map — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Map a meaningful regional comparison using licensed boundary data and cited measurements. Explain aggregation, missing regions, and collection dates; supply a sortable table and avoid exposing precise sensitive locations.
- **E12.** Trend chart — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Show how a relevant measure changes over time using cited observations. Display units, sample period, methodological changes, and missing intervals; avoid smoothing or truncated axes that create a misleading narrative.
- **E13.** Ranked comparison chart — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Compare alternatives using a bar or dot plot with an explicit shared measure. Explain eligibility and denominators, source every value, and separate editorial recommendations from the numerical ranking itself.
- **E14.** Distribution chart — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Reveal variation through a histogram, box plot, or quantile display when averages conceal useful differences. State sample size, grouping rules, and collection method; provide the source table and explain uncertainty.
- **E15.** Relationship scatter plot — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Explore whether two measured variables move together, with visible units and meaningful point labels. Explain sample selection and confounders, cite the dataset, and avoid implying that a fitted line establishes causation.
- **E16.** Composition breakdown — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Explain a budget, resource mix, or other genuine whole through stacked bars or a compact composition chart. Verify totals, identify rounding, label denominators, and provide numerical values without relying on color alone.
- **E17.** Uncertainty visual — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Make confidence intervals, estimate ranges, and conflicting study results visible instead of hiding them in prose. Explain what each interval means, cite its derivation, and distinguish statistical uncertainty from scenario assumptions.
- **E18.** Scenario comparison — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Visualize how outcomes change under explicitly labeled assumptions using sensitivity curves or small multiples. Publish the formula and input ranges, identify forecasts as forecasts, and allow readers to inspect the underlying calculations.
- **E19.** Comparison matrix — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Present product capabilities, tradeoffs, or requirements in a scannable table with dated evidence. Use consistent criteria, disclose missing information, and retain semantic headers plus a readable narrow-screen presentation.
- **E20.** Calculator result graphic — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Turn reader inputs into an explained result with a small accompanying visualization. Show formulas, units, assumptions, and sources; validate inputs and offer an accessible text result and reset control.
- **E21.** Evidence provenance map — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Show how a central conclusion traces back through datasets, methods, and primary sources. Link each node to its citation entry, identify derivative reporting, and expose unresolved evidence gaps clearly.
- **E22.** Evidence strength ladder — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Help readers understand which findings rest on direct measurement, synthesis, expert judgment, or inference. Use a domain-appropriate explained rubric, avoid universal credibility scores, and attach citations to each evidence category.
- **E23.** Research summary infographic — HYBRID. Combine a small number of verified findings into an editorial infographic with deterministic typography and data marks. Generated ornament may support the theme, but every number, label, and source remains independently editable.
- **E24.** Visual glossary — HYBRID. Explain unfamiliar terminology through concise definitions, relationships, and consistent illustrations. Keep definitions in real text, cite specialized claims, and provide a linear reading order so the glossary also works without its diagram.
- **E25.** Misconception and correction panel — HYBRID. Pair a clearly labeled misconception with its evidence-based correction and a helpful visual explanation. Avoid amplifying unsupported sensational claims; cite the correction and explain important exceptions in nearby text.
- **E26.** Action checklist — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Convert the article’s conclusions into a practical sequence of checks, prerequisites, and completion criteria. Use semantic controls where interactive, preserve a printable version, and distinguish recommended practice from externally mandated requirements.
- **E27.** Risk or failure matrix — DETERMINISTIC DATA. Explain meaningful failure modes with cited evidence and explicitly defined impact categories. Use numeric probabilities only when supported, identify qualitative judgments, and connect each risk to a practical mitigation.
- **E28.** Worked artifact or template — AUTHENTIC CAPTURE / DETERMINISTIC DATA. Include an annotated example document, configuration, worksheet, or completed template that readers can reuse. Mark demonstration values, provide an accessible downloadable equivalent, and explain how to adapt it.
- **E29.** Visual taxonomy gallery — HYBRID. Teach a useful classification through consistent example cards that make categories easy to compare. Use authentic examples where factual identification matters, label generated exemplars, and describe distinguishing features with source-linked text.
- **E30.** Shareable takeaway card — HYBRID. Derive a concise social or newsletter visual from the article’s strongest supported insight. Keep qualifications, attribution, and a canonical link legible; crop existing approved artwork instead of commissioning redundant assets.
